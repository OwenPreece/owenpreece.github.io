1 Op-amp design · Simulation

Op-Amp Buffer for a Sensor

Problem: Design a simple buffer for a sensor modeled as 0.02sin(200πt) V with a 100 kΩ source resistance. The buffer must preserve the signal while driving a load that varies from 1 kΩ to 10 kΩ.

My contribution
I developed and evaluated a buffer configuration in SPICE, kept the schematic organized with clear node labels, and used simulation results to assess how the circuit handled the sensor's source resistance and the specified load.
Result
The simulated design showed the expected buffer behavior: it preserved the sensor signal while reducing the loading effect on the source. Add or retain your final simulation plot here as evidence of the output behavior.
What this demonstrates
Understanding of voltage buffering, source and load resistance, op-amp configuration, and the use of simulation to evaluate a design against its requirements.
2 Amplifier design · Filtering

Amplifier for a Light-Following Robot

Problem: Design an amplifier for a light-following robot using a sinusoidally varying flashlight signal, while reducing sensitivity to room light. The input is an AC current with 0.1 mA peak-to-peak amplitude connected to a 1 kΩ resistor to ground.

My contribution
I explored filter options and op-amp configurations, compared their behavior using NGspice, and organized the schematic so the signal path and important nodes were clear. I evaluated the output amplitude and average voltage against the design requirements.
Result
The simulated output met the target peak-to-peak amplitude of 1 V ±5% and the average-output requirement of less than 1 mV. The comparison of configurations helped me understand how gain and filtering affect the desired signal and unwanted components.
What this demonstrates
Amplifier design under power-supply and circuit constraints, filter selection, quantitative verification, and technical communication through a readable schematic.

05 · Reflection

Design Journey

What has changed in my approach to circuit design during EC EN 340.

What I can do now

At the beginning of the semester, I did not have the same ability to troubleshoot circuit-design problems systematically. I can now identify fundamental circuit issues, use measurements and data to understand why a circuit is producing an undesired result, and narrow the likely cause to specific components or stages on the board. Rather than treating an unexpected output as one general problem, I can use evidence to investigate where the problem is occurring.

How my approach has developed

I have become more comfortable connecting calculations, circuit models, simulation, and physical measurements. I am learning to compare what a design should do with what the actual circuit does, then use that difference to guide troubleshooting. The variety of projects has helped me see that circuit design involves more than producing a working schematic: it also requires selecting components, testing assumptions, interpreting data, and communicating results.

Next step: Continue improving the connection between predicted and measured circuit behavior. For each hardware project, document the relevant measurements, identify discrepancies, and explain how the evidence led to a specific design or component-level adjustment.
06 · BYU learning connection

Intellectually Enlarging

Connecting practical engineering work with a BYU Aim.

The variety of projects in EC EN 340 has been intellectually enlarging because each project requires me to approach electronic systems from a different perspective. I have worked with amplifier design, filtering, circuit simulation, PCB layout, LED characterization, and hardware troubleshooting. Seeing these topics in different contexts has expanded my understanding beyond memorizing individual circuit principles.

Building and testing circuits has also helped me understand engineering fundamentals in a more practical way. Concepts such as current, voltage, resistance, gain, filtering, and device models become more meaningful when I use them to make design decisions and then compare those decisions with actual measurements. This experience reflects the BYU Aim of becoming intellectually enlarged: I am learning to connect theory with real engineering problems, ask better questions about unexpected results, and use evidence to improve a design.
